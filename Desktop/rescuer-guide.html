Rescuer Guide

Everything you need to rehome responsibly 🏅

From health requirements to home visits — a complete guide to help you find the best forever homes for your rescues.

BEFORE YOU LIST

Health Requirements

Every animal listed on FurriHearts should meet minimum health standards before being rehomed. This protects the adopter, their existing pets, and the animal itself.

Vaccinated

Core vaccines: FVRCP for cats, DHPP for dogs. Keep records for handover.

Dewormed

Treat for internal parasites before listing. Repeat every 3 months for fosters.

Flea & Tick Treated

Apply appropriate prevention before the animal meets potential adopters.

Spayed / Neutered

Strongly recommended before adoption. If too young, note in the listing and agree to neuter post-adoption.

Vet Health Check

A basic examination to confirm the animal is healthy and fit for rehoming.

FIV/FeLV Tested (Cats)

Especially important if the adopter has other cats. Disclose results honestly in your listing.

💡

Keep all vet records

Provide a copy of vaccination records and medical history to the adopter at handover. This builds trust and gives the new vet a baseline.

YOUR LISTING

Creating a Good Listing

A great listing gets more applications from the right adopters. Here's what makes the difference.

📸

Use Clear, Well-Lit Photos

Listings with 5+ photos get far more applications. Show the animal's face, body, and personality. Natural light works best.

✍️

Let AI Help Your Write-Up

Use FurriHearts' AI writing tool to generate a compelling profile. Review and personalise it — you know your rescue best.

🏷️

Be Honest About Health & Behaviour

Disclose any medical conditions, quirks, or special needs. Honest listings result in better matches and fewer returns.

🏠

Specify Your Home Requirements

Be clear about what kind of home you're looking for — indoor only, secured balconies, no young children, etc.

THE PROCESS

Using the Questionnaire

FurriHearts provides a standardised questionnaire for all applicants. Here's how to use it effectively.

1

Review every answer carefully

Don't skim — the questionnaire reveals the adopter's lifestyle, home situation, experience, and intentions. Pay close attention to home type, window security, and time commitment.

2

Follow up on unclear answers

If an answer is vague, reach out before approving. It's better to ask than regret a placement. Is the balcony truly secured? Do all family members agree?

3

Approve, decline, or request more info

You have full control. Approve good matches, politely decline unsuitable ones, and always notify the applicant either way.

DUE DILIGENCE

Vetting Adopters

Beyond the questionnaire, here's how to make sure you're placing your rescue with the right family.

📱

Have a Call or Video Chat

A 10-minute call tells you a lot. Are they excited? Do they ask good questions? Have they thought about vet care and emergencies?

🏠

Do a Home Visit

Check windows and balconies are secured, space is appropriate, and all household members are present and enthusiastic. A video call tour is an acceptable alternative.

📋

Use an Adoption Contract

A signed contract sets expectations — return policy, indoor requirements, neuter agreement, and follow-up check-ins.

❤️

Trust Your Instincts

If something feels off, it probably is. You are the last line of defence for your rescue. It's okay to say no — even to a seemingly good applicant.

🚩 Red flags to watch for

Wants animal as a gift for someone else · Unwilling to do a home visit · Plans to keep animal outdoors · Household members haven't agreed · Refuses to sign adoption contract · Unsecured high-floor balcony · Dismissive of vet costs · History of returned adoptions without explanation

AFTER ADOPTION

Post-Adoption Follow Up

A good rescuer doesn't disappear after handover. A simple follow-up shows you care and helps ensure the adoption sticks.

📅

Week 1 Check-In

Send a quick message — is the animal eating? Settling in? Any concerns? This reassures the adopter and catches issues early.

🗓️

Month 1 Follow-Up

Ask for a photo update! Confirm the animal has had or scheduled its first vet visit.

📋

Remind of Return Policy

Better a safe return than an abandoned animal. Remind adopters they can always come back to you if things don't work out.

⭐

Ask for a Review

Happy adopters are your best advocates. A kind review on your FurriHearts profile helps future adopters trust you.
